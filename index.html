Why does resistance respond so differently to temperature?

02 · Metal Electron–Phonon Scattering R = ρL/A

Metal Wire ↗ R↑

Positive Temperature Coefficient · α > 0

  • Conduction electrons drift through a lattice of fixed positive ions.
  • Heating intensifies lattice vibrations (phonons) — ions shake harder.
  • More frequent collisions → shorter mean free path → electron–phonon scattering.
  • Resistivity climbs → resistance rises with T.
  • R(T) ≈ R₀[1 + α(T − T₀)]
↗ α > 0 · PTC
images/slide-2.png
03 · Carbon Grain-Boundary Conduction

Carbon Film → R≈

Near-Zero Coefficient · α ≈ 0

  • Thin carbon layer on a ceramic core — cheap, stable, common in circuits.
  • π-electrons hop between carbon grains at boundaries.
  • Phonon scattering increases with T — but grain effects partially cancel it.
  • Net: α ≈ 0 or slightly negative — R barely budges.
  • |α| is orders of magnitude smaller than pure metals.
→ α ≈ −50 to −200 ppm/°C
images/slide-3.png
04 · Semi Band-Gap Physics n ∝ e^(−Eg/2kT)

Semiconductor ↘ R↓

Negative Temperature Coefficient · α < 0

  • A band gap Eg separates valence and conduction bands.
  • Cold → few carriers cross the gap → high resistivity.
  • Heat supplies kT → electrons jump Eg → carrier density n surges.
  • Extra carriers outweigh increased scattering.
  • ρ(T) ∝ e^(Eg/2kT)
↘ α < 0 · NTC
images/slide-4.png
05 · NTC Engineered Sensitivity

Thermistor (NTC) ↘↘

Exponential Resistance Drop

  • Semiconductor ceramic (metal oxides) — designed for huge α.
  • Thermal carrier generation dominates everything else.
  • n rises exponentially → resistance plummets with T.
  • R(T) = R₀ · eβ(1/T − 1/T₀)
  • Far more sensitive than ordinary semiconductors — built to sense temperature.
↘↘ β ≈ 3000–5000 K

07 · Applied Where It Matters

Real-World Applications

Thermistors in Sensors

  • Digital thermometers, HVAC, battery protection — anywhere ΔR/ΔT must be large.
  • Simple voltage divider → temperature readout, no fancy ADC needed.
  • 3D printer hotends, ovens, medical fever monitors.

Metal Wires in Precision Resistors

  • Manganin / Constantan wire-wound: tiny α, stable across wide T range.
  • Wheatstone bridges, calibration standards, precision measurement.
  • Predictable T drift → correction tables, not compensation circuits.
